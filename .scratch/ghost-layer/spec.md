# Ghost Layer — Spec

Status: ready-for-agent

Source: the [Wayfinder map](map.md) and its 16 resolved tickets in `issues/`. Vocabulary: `GLOSSARY.md`. ADR: [Aigis plans only between Missions](../../docs/adr/0001-aigis-plans-only-between-missions.md). Where this spec and the draft pitch disagree, this spec wins.

## Problem Statement

The brief is a short game that uses **game AI**: behaviour trees, utility AI and profiling, with no ML or LLMs. It is built solo in Godot 4.7 in about six weeks (build from 2026-10-12, delivered around 2026-11-19) as a playable Windows build, presented live, plus a written account of the AI.

The usual way to show enemy adaptation, "the enemies got harder", is invisible and unconvincing. An evaluator watching a live demo can't tell a learning opponent from a difficulty slider. The player needs to *see* that the enemy noticed how they play and answered it, and the presenter needs to show the same thing on demand in about ten minutes.

## Solution

**Ghost Layer** is a third-person stealth game in a modern-retro PSone look. The player is an Operative of Metis, a mercenary team hired by a whistleblower to find out what Aigis is: Palladion's tactical intelligence, which defends a coastal compound.

Over three Missions and a final Mission at the Core, Aigis profiles the Operative's Habits and, between Missions, deploys Countermeasures that the player can see and hear: cameras, helmets, vests, patrol pairs, sentries, HQ polls, radio callbacks. Its five Voices plan these openly in a Briefing the player overhears, naming the Operative's habits. The post-Mission screen shows Aigis's Profile as a strip of icons. The player can also deceive the guards with Spoofed transmissions made from captured Voiceprints, which Glaukopis judges for authenticity. A silent Lethality tally decides the Dominant Voice, which colours every Briefing and decides which of three endings plays.

The AI design has three layers. Guards run a behaviour tree. Aigis is a deterministic utility AI that acts only between Missions. Profiling feeds Aigis. A Free mode with Presets and an F3 debug overlay lets the presenter show any Profile, any deployment and any ending live, and a short Spanish write-up documents the AI using deployment tables generated by the test suite.

## User Stories

### Movement, camera and stealth

1. As a player, I want an over-the-shoulder third-person camera that never gets stuck in walls, so that I can read the space around the Operative.
2. As a player, I want to swap the camera shoulder with Tab, so that I can see past a wall that crowds one side.
3. As a player, I want the Operative's body to hide when the camera is pressed very close, so that it never blocks my view.
4. As a player, I want WASD to run by default and Shift to walk quietly, so that I choose between speed and noise.
5. As a player, I want to toggle crouch with C or Ctrl, so that I can hide behind low cover and be seen more slowly.
6. As a player, I want guards to see me with a front cone and a shorter peripheral cone, so that sneaking behind or beside them is a real tactic.
7. As a player, I want low cover to block sight to my chest when I crouch, so that cover works as I expect.
8. As a player, I want guards to notice me faster when I'm close, standing, running or in the open, so that how I move matters.
9. As a player, I want running footsteps to be heard within 6 m, so that I slow down near guards.
10. As a player, I want noise to make guards suspicious and never alert them by itself, so that a mistake is recoverable.
11. As a player, I want guards to go from patrol to suspicion to alert to evasion and back to patrol, so that every encounter has a readable arc.
12. As a player, I want a "?" over a suspicious guard and a "!" over an alerted one, so that I know each guard's state at a glance.
13. As a player, I want arcs around the screen centre pointing at each guard who is noticing me, growing and warming in colour, so that I can tell where the danger is coming from and how close it is.
14. As a player, I want a red screen-edge flash and a radio call when a guard raises the alert, so that I know the whole Area is now hunting me.
15. As a player, I want alerted guards to search my last known position during evasion and then give up, so that I can escape and recover.
16. As a player, I want an ALERT/EVASION timer on the HUD, so that I know how long the danger lasts.
17. As a player, I want guards that walk around obstacles cleanly, so that their movement looks deliberate, not broken.
18. As a player, I want to knock (V) anywhere, with a short cooldown, to draw nearby guards, so that I can pull them into position.

### Combat verbs

19. As a player, I want a suppressed tranquilizer pistol, so that I can neutralize guards without killing them.
20. As a player, I want a tranq headshot to neutralize at once and a body shot to take about 3 s while the guard stumbles and can't radio, so that precision is rewarded.
21. As a player, I want a suppressed assault rifle whose suppressor breaks after 10 shots, so that lethal force has a stealth budget.
22. As a player, I want a rifle headshot or two body hits to kill, so that the rifle feels decisive.

24. As a player, I want to aim with RMB and fire with LMB only while aiming, so that every shot is deliberate.
25. As a player, I want to switch weapons with 1/2 and reload with R (and automatically on an empty magazine), so that ammo management is simple.
26. As a player, I want resupply crates that restore ammo, the suppressor and my items, so that a long Mission stays playable.
27. As a player, I want a choke takedown (E) from behind on a guard who isn't alerted, so that I can neutralize up close without a gun.
28. As a player, I want a kill takedown on its own key (X), so that I never kill by accident.
29. As a player, I want a prompt "E choke · X kill" when I'm in range, so that both options are always clear.
30. As a player, I want to pick up a Down guard over my shoulder and drop them with E, so that I can hide bodies out of sight lines.

### Items

## Implementation Decisions

### Platform and dependencies

- Godot 4.7, Forward Plus, Jolt physics, Windows export. Keyboard and mouse only.
- **LimboAI** (GDExtension) for the guard behaviour tree. Smoke-test it on 4.7 on day 1; **Beehave** is the fallback. Built-in `SpringArm3D` and `NavigationAgent3D`; the PSX shaders are written by hand. No Dialogue Manager.
- Tests use godot_ai's **McpTestSuite**, run from the tests folder at the project root.
- Assets: Quaternius characters and animations (QAL licence: no standalone redistribution; raw files stay in `_raw/`), Kenney as a CC0 fallback, military props built by hand, Screaming Brain Tiny Texture Pack 2 (CC0), Press Start 2P + VT323 (OFL), Kenney/OpenGameArt CC0 sounds, Pexels/Pixabay stock footage for the ending only. Mixamo is not used. Every asset gets a credits line.
- Prototype code: leaf pieces are lifted whole (the PSX shader and post-process, the two-spring-arm camera rig, the detection arc widget). Anything that crosses systems (guard perception and state, Spoofing, alert propagation) is rewritten, reusing the tuned numbers below. The prototypes live on the `prototype/stealth-core`, `prototype/spoofing` and `prototype/psx-look` branches.

### Architecture (ADR-0001)

Three layers. Each pure module is node-free and deterministic; scenes gather facts, call the module, and only read its results.

1. **Guards:** a LimboAI behaviour tree per guard. It reads a pure **guard alert state machine** module for its state.
2. **Glaukopis:** a pure in-Mission **Spoofing judge**. It is the only Aigis logic in the field and is unaffected by Voice weights.
3. **Aigis:** a pure, between-Missions **campaign module** that owns the Profile and Lethality rules and the Voices' utility planning.

Adding Voice behaviour inside a Mission later would be a new seam (feeding weights into the guard tree), not a tweak.

### Pure module 1: guard alert state machine

Interface shape from the prototype: `tick(delta, seen_rate, seen_pos, why, at_target)`, `hear(...)`, `radio(...)`; each returns a reason string for the debug overlay. States: patrol → suspicion → alert → evasion → patrol.

- Meter at 0.35 = suspicion (shows "?", a 1 s turn-and-look beat, then investigate, then a 5 s look-around). Full meter = alert ("!", radio call alerts every guard). Drains 0.25/s. Alert is lost after 3 s unseen. Evasion searches the last known position for 12 s; seeing the Operative there means alert again.
- Fill rate (computed by the scene, passed in as `seen_rate`): front cone 30° half-angle × 16 m, plus a peripheral cone 60° × 6 m at half rate. Line of sight is a ray to the chest. Multipliers: crouched ×0.5; still ×0.6, walking ×1, running ×1.5; closer than 3 m ×4; faster with less distance. A carried body makes the Operative count as standing and walking.
- Hearing: running footsteps 6 m, knock and Magazine landing 10 m, loud shot 40 m, noisy choke (Takedowns L3) 6 m. No wall occlusion. Noise means suspicion at the source, never alert.
- Down guard seen (same meter): suspicion → walk over and check → radio an alert if dead, or wake them if Neutralized (the woken guard joins in suspicion and remembers nothing). Downs L2 replaces the check with an instant alert.
- Security cameras use the same meter (front cone only); a full meter is a radio alert with no suspicion step.

### Guard behaviour and combat rules

- Navigation: bake the navmesh in the editor with a radius margin above the body's 0.35 m radius; use `path_height_offset`, not a large `path_desired_distance` (the prototype's workaround made guards cut corners into obstacles); consider turning on avoidance.
- Takedown: only on a guard who isn't alerted, within ~1.5 m, with the Operative outside the guard's front cone. Choke: 2 s, Operative exposed, Neutralizes. Kill: 0.5 s.
- Tranq: 4-dart magazine, 8 total, suppressor never breaks. Headshot Neutralizes at once; a body shot Neutralizes after ~3 s (the guard stumbles, is suspicious, and can't radio).
- Rifle: 30-round magazine, 60 total. One headshot or two body hits kill. The suppressor breaks after 10 shots; after that, shots are loud. Hitscan. Reload ~1.5 s, silent, automatic when empty; no reload while carrying.
- Neutralized guards wake after ~90 s; choking again resets the timer.
- Carry: E toggles it. A fixed pose attached to the shoulder bone, no ragdoll. Walk speed (slower crouched); one-handed tranq with worse accuracy; no Magazine throw.
- Open combat: guards shoot back, with hit chance falling with distance and when the Operative runs. The Operative dies after 4 hits and regenerates after 5 s without damage. Death restores the last Checkpoint.
- Knock: anywhere, 1 s cooldown.
- Every guard makes spontaneous radio check-ins every 20–30 s. They serve only as flavour and a Voiceprint source.
- All numbers here are tuned during the build.

### Pure module 2: Glaukopis (Spoofing judge)

`judge(facts, commit) -> {doubt, exposed, reasons}`. The scene gathers the facts. Deterministic doubt points; exposed at **40+** (**25+** with Spoofing L1):

| Fact | Doubt |
|---|---|
| Voiceprint already flagged | +100 |
| Speaker is the recipient | +100 |
| A recipient can see the speaker | +60 |
| Any guard knows the speaker is Down | +60 |
| "All clear" while a recipient sees the Operative | +100 |
| "All clear" < 15 s after an alert | +30 |
| Speaker busy searching | +30 for "all clear", +15 for other orders |
| Recipient is All units | +10 |
| Contradicts an order the same guard got < 30 s ago | +30 |
| Each transmission in the last 60 s | +15 |

- Orders: go check a place, hold position, all clear/stand down, **Report in** (answers a pending HQ poll as the polled guard and needs that guard's Voiceprint). Four named places per Area.
- Exposed: the Voiceprint is flagged for good; the recipients search a spot within ±5 m of where the Operative transmitted from; the recipient says the "You don't sound right" line. There is no other feedback to the player. Doubt and reasons go only to the debug overlay.
- Spoofing L2 callback: after a transmission, the recipient radios the speaker. A live speaker who is up denies it, which exposes the transmission. A Down speaker doesn't answer (the +60 rule applies only if some guard knows they're Down).
- Spoofing L3: a Voiceprint is flagged after one use, whether or not it was exposed.
- Capture: within 6 m during 2.5 s of a check-in; partial progress carries over between check-ins. Voiceprints never carry between Missions and roll back with Checkpoints.
- Radio UI: a radial menu with time slowed (speaker → recipient → order → place → confirm). The shape and slow-motion factor are tuned during the build.

### Pure module 3: Aigis campaign module (Profile, Lethality, Voices)

This is one seam. A **campaign record** (Profile points per Habit, Lethality, Lethality denominator, next Mission index) plus a **Mission tally** go in; a new record, a Mission plan and an ending come out.

- `complete_mission(record, tally) -> record` folds a completed Mission into the record:
  - Habit points are clamped MGSV-style to cap × 100 + 99. The cap is per Mission: **0 / 1 / 2 / 3** (Missions 1, 2, 3, Core). Levels = points ÷ 100.
  - **Stealth and Combat tug of war:** the Mission decides one tendency (whichever side leads by a margin), which adds to that side and subtracts from the other. A Mission completed with no alert counts as Stealth.
  - Decay of at most ½ level per Mission for the other four Habits when they weren't used.
  - Lethality adds the Mission's weighted deaths (a guard 1, a Target 3; killing a Neutralized guard counts in full). The denominator adds the guards **actually spawned** in the Mission (extras and reinforcements included) plus 3 per Target.
  - An aborted or quit Mission is never folded in.
- `plan_mission(profile, lethality, mission_index) -> {weights, dominant, budget, pinned, picks[{step, sponsor, score, candidates}], briefing_keys}`:
  - Levels **unlock** Countermeasure steps, and the plan **deploys** them. The budget counts response levels: **0 / 2 / 4 / 6**. Steps deploy in order up each Habit's ladder. Unspent budget is fine.
  - **Pin:** the strongest Habit (highest level, ties broken by more points) gets its full unlocked response first.
  - **Greedy fill:** the candidates are each Habit's next unlocked step. Score = sponsor Voice weight × that Habit's points. Take the highest; break ties in Habit-table order.
  - **Weights:** the Dominant Voice is 2.0, every other Voice 1.0.
  - Fully deterministic: the same inputs always give the same plan.
- `resolve_ending(lethality) -> band`. **Lethality ratio** = Lethality ÷ denominator; undefined before Mission 1 (the band is then Pallas). Bands: **< 0.10 Pallas, 0.10–0.40 Nike, > 0.40 Gorgo**; a value exactly on a threshold goes to the higher band. The thresholds are tuned during the build.
- Accepted side effect: Lethality leaks into deployment (a lethal campaign gets more Gorgo gear), while the number stays silent and separate from the Profile.

**Habits and Countermeasure ladders** (sponsor Voice in brackets):

| Habit | Measured by | L1 | L2 | L3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stealth | guards put Down while no alert is active; a Mission with no alert | security cameras [Glaukopis] | one extra patrol [Promachos] | overwatch sentries on high points [Gorgo] |
| Combat | alerts raised, loud shots, kills during an alert | vests [Gorgo] | +2 guards [Promachos] | 2 reinforcements from the road entry, once per alert [Promachos] |
| Headshots | tranq or rifle headshots | helmets on 33% [Gorgo] | 66% [Gorgo] | 100% [Gorgo] |
| Takedowns | chokes and kills from behind | guards turn around more often [Pallas] | patrols in pairs [Gorgo] | choke takes 3 s and makes noise within 6 m; the kill stays at 0.5 s [Nike] |
| Downs | Down guards found, Neutralized guards woken | random HQ poll [Pallas] | a found Down guard means an instant alert [Nike] | targeted HQ poll [Pallas] |
| Spoofing | Spoofed transmissions sent, extra weight if exposed | threshold 40 → 25 [Glaukopis] | callback [Glaukopis] | single-use Voiceprints [Glaukopis] |

- Helmet: a headshot counts as a body hit. Vest: stops tranq body shots; rifle body hits needed go from 2 to 4. Cameras: the rifle destroys them, the tranq does nothing, and a destroyed camera is treated as a missing guard.
- HQ poll L1: about every 45 s, "<X>, report" to one random living guard, weighted toward guards who are Down. No answer within ~10 s sends the nearest guard to X's last position in suspicion. L3: polls the guard nobody has seen for the longest, using the guards' sight check run at a low rate on each other (fallback if that's too expensive: a random poll every 20 s).
- Trigger point values are tuned during the build and checked by a test that simulates several Missions.

### Mission state, Checkpoints and saves

- A Mission's live state (Habit tally, Lethality so far, Voiceprints) is held apart from the campaign record. It is snapshotted at Checkpoints and folded in only on completion.
- **Checkpoint** (in memory only): objective step, Operative position, ammo, Scanner charges, Magazines, the Mission's Habit tally and Lethality, Voiceprints and flags, which guards are dead and where bodies lie. On restore, the dead stay dead and bodies stay put; every living guard (Neutralized ones too) returns to their patrol post with the alert cleared; Countermeasures stay as deployed.
- **Campaign slot file** (three slots): next Mission index, Profile points per Habit, Lethality, denominator, a complete flag, and the time last saved. No version field; a missing or unreadable file is an empty slot. Briefings, deployment and the Dominant Voice are rebuilt by `plan_mission`. A separate settings file survives New game.
- Writes happen only after the post-Mission screen commits the points. The Core is written at the terminal, before the finale, and marks the slot complete.
- Free mode writes nothing. Custom starts from Clean slate.

- Targets are always required: killed on the spot (the objective completes), or Neutralized and carried to the Extraction point. They use the guard model with a distinct silhouette (a cap or beret and a different colour), the same tree, a fixed 2–3-point route and one escort. They are unarmed and run to a safe room on alert until evasion ends. A carried Target is a Down guard if spotted.

### Narrative delivery

- English source plus a Spanish translation in one Godot CSV translation table (`keys,en,es`), `tr(key)` everywhere, defaulting to the OS locale and switchable live. Keys are namespaced by use (e.g. `brief.pallas.habit.headshots`, `chatter.headshots.2.warn.a`, `codec.m2.03`, `end.gorgo.04`). Only proper names are slotted in (`{target}`); a line that names a Habit gets one full line per Habit.
- A Codec call is plain data: an ordered list of `[speaker, key]`. One small custom player handles both kinds of call: **paused full-screen** (two portraits, frequency, typewriter text) and **live** (Peace Walker subtitles over a `CALL >> <speaker>` bar). Code chooses which lines play (`briefing_keys`, band × deaths, Habit variants). Calls are scripted only; the player can't call Metis.
- Metis: a **handler** (objectives, tutorial) and an **analyst** (intel, dossiers, the Aigis thread). The Operative gives terse replies in calls and never speaks in the field. Portraits are in-engine renders put through the PSX pass.
- **Briefing** (3–4 lines, non-diegetic): Pallas names the top 1–2 Habits; for each answered Habit, the sponsor of its highest deployed step hints at the answer; the Dominant Voice closes. Nike adds a line when Takedowns L3 is deployed. Mission 1 gets only the closing line.
- **Post-Mission screen:** the 0–3 icon strip with rise/fall arrows (no raw numbers, no Lethality) plus one Dominant Voice line (band × whether this Mission's Lethality was 0; 6 lines). There is no Metis debrief.
- **Guard Profile chatter:** a warning at 50 points before a level, and a confirmation once a Countermeasure is deployed (also used for Spoofing ladder changes). Two variants per set.
- **Field text:** radio traffic (check-ins, HQ polls, Report in, callbacks, relay offline, exposure lines) appears as bottom subtitles with the speaker's name, heard anywhere: the last two lines, fading after ~6 s. Guard-to-guard chatter appears only within hearing range. Targets get 2–3 chatter lines with their escort; the director gets one line to Aigis.
- **Story beats** come through intel pickups plus 1–2 analyst lines: M1 the whistleblower client; M2 Synthetic Content Inflation; M3 dead commanders' memories, the Core under the Harbour, and Aigis about to get full authority; Core: feed it fabricated memories.
- **Finale:** a scripted last Spoofed transmission ("Transmit memories", radial, slowed time, can't fail) → **Final debate** (a full-screen text scene; each Voice has a glyph and one line per band in character; Glaukopis's line varies with Spoofing level 0–3; the Dominant Voice speaks last) → **Ending** (4–6 lines per band; the last line addressed to the Operative) → **Epilogue** (a paused Codec call, ~4 lines: Metis profits from the war). Stock footage appears only here (PSX-processed stills with pan/zoom, at most 2–3 short `.ogv` loops).
- Budget: **3,500 source words** (~225 narrative lines + ~80 UI strings). The user writes the Voices and endings; an agent drafts the rest and the Spanish, and the user proofreads it.

### Look, HUD and input

- 3D at **854×480** with nearest upscaling; no vertex snapping or affine textures; per-pixel lighting with shadows; a full-screen 15-bit banding pass (31 levels per channel, no dither) under the HUD; far depth fog (20–60 m); 128 px nearest textures on world-space box UVs (~2 m per tile), with geometry subdivided every ~2 m.
- HUD laid out on a 640×360 logical canvas at native resolution. **HUD E:** left, a vertical life bar, "OPERATIVE" and a slot number; right strip, ALERT/EVASION + timer, a stance silhouette, an item card (name + count/charges) and a weapon card (pip column for the magazine, suppressor count, reserve). A compass ring top-right with no enemy dots. A "VP A B" Voiceprint line. The MGSV arcs and ?/! marks. The radial, codec and post-Mission screen share E's white/grey palette. Press Start 2P for labels, VT323 for text.

## Testing Decisions

- **A good test checks external behaviour only:** given inputs at a module's public interface, assert the outputs a player or the overlay would see (deployed steps, exposure, state and reason). Never assert private fields or call order. Tests use fixed inputs and never run a scene.
- **Three seams, all pure and node-free, tested with godot_ai's McpTestSuite** (from the tests folder; assertions like `assert_eq`, `assert_true`, `assert_has_key`):
  1. **Aigis campaign module** (`complete_mission`, `plan_mission`, `resolve_ending`). Covers: cap and clamp per Mission; carried points clamped by the next Mission's cap (Mission 1 carries up to 199, Mission 2 up to 299, Mission 3 and the Core up to 399); the tug of war; decay; aborted Missions applying nothing; the pin rule; greedy fill order, scores and Habit-table tie-breaks; budget 0/2/4/6 and steps deploying in order; band thresholds, including exact-threshold values and the undefined ratio; the Dominant Voice weight; briefing keys; determinism (same inputs, same plan); a multi-Mission simulation that checks the trigger point tuning reaches the intended levels. A **Preset × Mission suite writes the deployment tables** as Markdown for the write-up.
  2. **Glaukopis** (`judge`). One test per doubt rule, the 40 and 25 thresholds, flagging on exposure, single-use flagging at Spoofing L3, and that `commit = false` changes nothing.
  3. **Guard alert state machine.** Transitions and timings (0.35 suspicion, 0.25/s drain, 3 s to lose the alert, 12 s evasion), noise never alerting, radio alerting, Down-guard checking vs Downs L2 instant alert, camera alert with no suspicion step, and reason strings.
- **Not unit-tested:** scenes, the behaviour tree wiring, perception rays, navigation, HUD, the codec player, Checkpoint restore and save files. These are verified by playtesting in Free mode with the F3 overlay, against the Milestone 1 "done when" and each week's slice.
- **Prior art:** none on main yet. The prototypes on `prototype/stealth-core` (`proto_alert`) and `prototype/spoofing` (`proto_glaukopis`) show the node-free shapes these seams follow. godot_ai's McpTestSuite is the only test base.

## Out of Scope

- Machine learning or LLMs; Voice-driven behaviour inside a Mission (ADR-0001).
- Base management (Mother Base), squad control or switching Metis members, multiplayer/co-op.
- Voice acting, 3D cinematics, stock footage outside the ending.
- Gamepad support and key rebinding.
- Extra Missions (the Relay station and Annex revisits) and the favourite-flank Habit.
- Light and night as a stealth factor (there is no light model) and a night Habit.
- A counter item or dispatch missions (no Chicken Hat), rations, a loud weapon, a seventh Habit, and Countermeasures aimed at items.
- Environmental kills and explosives; a hold-up verb; dragging bodies.
- Branching dialogue, Dialogue Manager, and a "spare me" Target scene.
- Replaying completed campaign Missions; deleting save slots; a save version field.
- Rescue objectives; optional Targets.
- PSX vertex snapping and affine texture warping.
